On follows the old lunisolar calendar, 旧暦 kyūreki: each issue opens at a new moon and takes the old name of its month. Each moon is drawn as it looks at 9 pm in Japan, waxing on the right; the number under it is the date, and a kanji marks the first day of a solar term.

Each moon is drawn as it looks at 9 pm in Japan, waxing on the right. The small number above it is the night of the lunar month; the date below is the day it falls on. A kanji under a date marks the first day of one of the 24 solar terms. Touch or point at a night for its phase, its kō and the times of the new moon, quarters and full moon. A month’s name links to its issue once that issue is out.

Months begin on the day of the new moon, Japan time, and take their number from the principal solar term that falls in them; a month without one is a leap month (閏月 urūzuki). Calculated for On from the positions of the sun and moon. Where a new moon or solar term falls close to midnight, a printed calendar may differ by a day.
